Chiller Types We Service

01

Centrifugal Chillers

Centrifugal chillers use high-speed impellers to compress refrigerant and are the workhorses of large-tonnage commercial cooling. Common in hospitals, data centers, and campus facilities above 200 tons, they offer high efficiency at full load and require rigorous attention to refrigerant charge, bearing condition, and approach temperatures to maintain rated performance.

02

Screw Chillers

Screw chillers use counter-rotating helical rotors and perform exceptionally well across a wide range of partial loads — making them well-suited for manufacturing, industrial processes, and large commercial buildings with variable occupancy. Gardner Air services both oil-flooded and oil-free screw configurations and performs oil analysis as a standard component of preventative maintenance visits.

03

Scroll Chillers

Scroll chillers operate in smaller commercial tonnages and are common in mid-size office buildings, retail centers, and light industrial facilities. Their simpler mechanical design does not eliminate the need for disciplined maintenance: refrigerant charge verification, condenser coil condition, and controls calibration all require periodic attention to hold efficiency.

04

Absorption Chillers

Absorption chillers use a heat source — typically steam, hot water, or direct-fired natural gas — rather than a compressor to drive the refrigeration cycle. They are found in facilities with waste heat recovery systems, cogeneration plants, and district energy applications. Gardner Air's service approach covers solution concentration, crystallization prevention, purge system operation, and heat exchanger condition.

05

Air-Cooled vs. Water-Cooled

Air-cooled chillers reject heat through ambient-air condensers and are common where cooling tower infrastructure is not feasible. Water-cooled chillers pair with a cooling tower and achieve higher efficiency over the full operating season. Each configuration carries distinct maintenance requirements: condenser coil fouling for air-cooled units; tower water treatment, tube fouling, and approach temperature monitoring for water-cooled systems. Gardner Air services both.

Diagnostics

Common Chiller Issues We Diagnose

Root-cause diagnosis before any intervention. Gardner Air’s technicians are trained to identify what is actually wrong — not to replace parts sequentially until the problem resolves.

Refrigerant leaks

Even a small refrigerant loss shifts the evaporator pressure and reduces capacity. Gardner Air uses electronic leak detection and performs a full system leak survey rather than topping off the charge without identifying the source.

Low evaporator pressure

Below-design evaporator pressure typically signals a refrigerant undercharge, a partially closed isolation valve, or fouled evaporator tubes. Each cause requires a different corrective action, which is why accurate diagnosis before any intervention matters.

Compressor issues

Centrifugal compressor surge, elevated vibration, or abnormal bearing temperatures on screw compressors are early indicators of mechanical wear. Catching these in a maintenance visit avoids the much larger cost of a compressor failure mid-season.

Condenser fouling

Scaled or biofilm-fouled condenser tubes raise condensing pressure, reduce efficiency, and accelerate wear throughout the refrigerant circuit. Water-cooled chillers require tube cleaning at defined intervals; air-cooled condenser coils require fin cleaning and inspection.

Oil and lubrication issues

Contaminated oil, low oil pressure, or oil that has degraded beyond specification damages compressor bearings and can travel into the refrigerant circuit. Oil sampling and analysis on every preventative maintenance visit gives Gardner Air a trend line — not just a snapshot.

Control and sensor faults

A chiller running on inaccurate sensor data or mis-calibrated controls operates inefficiently and may short-cycle or trip on safety limits. Gardner Air's technicians verify sensor accuracy and controls programming as part of every scheduled visit.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Commercial Chiller Service

How often should a commercial chiller be serviced?

Commercial chillers should receive a comprehensive preventative maintenance inspection at minimum once per year, with most facilities in Southern California scheduling quarterly visits given year-round cooling demand. Higher-criticality applications — data centers, hospitals, and 24/7 manufacturing operations — often warrant more frequent inspections. The correct interval depends on the chiller's age, refrigerant type, operating hours, and how much unplanned downtime the facility can tolerate. A maintenance program scoped to the equipment is more reliable than a calendar default.

What is the difference between centrifugal and screw chillers?

Centrifugal chillers use a high-speed rotating impeller to compress refrigerant vapor and perform most efficiently at or near full load. They are the standard choice for large-tonnage applications above roughly 200 tons. Screw chillers use meshing helical rotors and maintain strong efficiency across a wider range of partial loads, making them well-suited for facilities with variable cooling demand. Both types require regular oil analysis, refrigerant charge verification, and controls calibration, but the specific service procedures and wear points differ between the two designs.

How long do commercial chillers typically last?

Commercial chillers maintained on a disciplined preventative maintenance program typically last 20 to 25 years. Chillers that receive irregular or incomplete maintenance often require major component replacement or full replacement well before that range. The compressor, heat exchangers, and controls are the primary cost drivers in older equipment. A documented maintenance history also informs the replacement-vs.-repair decision when a major component fails late in the unit's life.

What are signs my chiller needs immediate service?

Indicators that a chiller requires prompt attention include: high discharge pressure alarms, low evaporator pressure faults, unusual compressor noise or vibration, unexplained loss of cooling capacity, refrigerant leak alarms, and oil pressure faults. Any of these conditions left unaddressed can escalate from a serviceable issue to a compressor failure or system shutdown. Facilities with Gardner Air maintenance agreements receive priority scheduling when these conditions are reported.
