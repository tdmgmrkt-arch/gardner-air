Why Specialized Service Matters

The Controls Layer Determines Whether Your System Runs to Design Intent

The controls layer is the intelligence that determines whether a commercial HVAC system operates efficiently, reliably, and within the setpoints a facility manager specified. A chiller plant or air handling system with degraded controls is not operating to its design intent — it is operating to whatever the sensors are currently reading and whatever the control logic last stored, which may be neither accurate nor current.

Gardner Air services the controls and building automation systems that govern commercial HVAC equipment across Riverside County, San Bernardino County, and Orange County. That spans BACnet DDC systems in corporate campuses and healthcare facilities, Modbus integration in industrial plants, legacy LonWorks networks in older institutional buildings, and cloud-connected platforms in multi-site commercial portfolios.

The common thread across all of them: controls problems are rarely self-announcing. Sensor drift, communication faults, and incorrect PID tuning degrade system performance gradually — until a regulatory inspection, an energy spike, or an equipment failure forces the issue.

For facilities managers, the building automation systemis both the monitoring layer and the intervention point. Gardner Air’s approach to controls service treats it as both — maintenance that verifies the system is reading accurately, and diagnostics that investigate what the data is revealing about the underlying equipment.

Controls System Types We Service

01

BACnet Building Automation Systems

BACnet (Building Automation and Control Networks) is the dominant open-protocol standard for commercial building controls. It enables communication between HVAC controllers, meters, lighting systems, and other building subsystems from multiple manufacturers. Gardner Air services BACnet networks at the controller level — verifying network addressing, object configuration, alarm logic, and integration with field devices. Clients on BACnet platforms can expect their systems to be maintained against the configuration documentation, not just observed for obvious faults.

02

LonWorks Controls

LonWorks is a peer-to-peer control networking protocol found in institutional buildings, campuses, and transportation facilities — often in systems installed before BACnet became the dominant commercial standard. LonWorks networks in older buildings require specific diagnostic tools and protocol knowledge that general HVAC contractors do not carry. Gardner Air services LonWorks control systems including node commissioning, network variable binding verification, and integration with modern supervisory platforms where legacy-to-current migration is in progress.

03

Modbus Integration

Modbus is a serial communication protocol widely used in industrial HVAC, building controls, and process equipment. It is common in facilities that operate variable-frequency drives, chillers, boilers, and packaged equipment alongside a supervisory BAS. Gardner Air services Modbus register mapping, communication fault diagnosis, and integration with higher-level control platforms — ensuring that the data flowing from Modbus devices to the BAS is accurate and that command signals are executing correctly at the field device level.

04

Direct Digital Controls (DDC)

Direct digital controls replace pneumatic and analog control with microprocessor-based controllers that execute sequences of operation for individual HVAC equipment — air handling units, VAV boxes, fan coil units, and terminal units. DDC controllers require periodic verification that control sequences are executing correctly, sensor inputs are calibrated, and PID loop parameters are tuned to current system conditions. Gardner Air's DDC service scope covers controller programming verification, sensor calibration, sequence of operations testing, and alarm logic review.

05

Wireless and Cloud-Connected Controls

Wireless and cloud-connected controls platforms extend BAS visibility to facilities without extensive hardwired infrastructure and enable centralized monitoring across multi-site portfolios. Gardner Air supports integration of wireless sensor and control nodes with existing BAS platforms, configuration of cloud dashboard access, and verification of data integrity between field devices and the monitoring layer. For facilities moving from standalone controls to a connected platform, Gardner Air manages the integration process to ensure existing HVAC equipment is represented accurately in the new system.

Diagnostics

Common Controls Issues We Diagnose

Root-cause diagnosis before any intervention. Gardner Air’s technicians are trained to trace a fault across protocol boundaries — from a field sensor through a Modbus device to a BACnet controller to the supervisory BAS.

Sensor drift and calibration loss

Temperature, pressure, humidity, and CO2 sensors drift from their calibration baseline over time. A sensor reading 4°F above actual supply air temperature causes the control system to overcool spaces, increasing energy consumption and reducing occupant comfort. Gardner Air calibrates sensors against reference instruments on every maintenance visit and documents the calibration data as a trend record.

Communication faults between controllers

BACnet, Modbus, and LonWorks networks experience communication faults from hardware failures, network topology changes, address conflicts, and firmware incompatibilities. A controller that has dropped off the network is not alarming — it is silently executing the last command it received, which may be a damper open, a setpoint at its limit, or a disabled safety interlock.

Incorrect PID tuning causing cycling

PID control loops that are not tuned to the current system's thermal characteristics cause equipment to overshoot setpoints, short-cycle, and hunt continuously for a stable operating point. This accelerates mechanical wear and produces energy inefficiency. Gardner Air evaluates PID performance on every maintenance visit and retunes loops where system response data indicates instability.

Unauthorized setpoint changes

Commercial controls systems with multiple users or operators frequently accumulate unauthorized or undocumented setpoint changes that shift the system away from its intended design conditions. Gardner Air's maintenance visits include a setpoint audit — comparing current programmed setpoints against the documented design intent and flagging deviations for the facilities manager's review.

Expired firmware and security vulnerabilities

Building automation systems that have not received firmware updates carry known security vulnerabilities and may no longer be supported by manufacturer patches. In healthcare, pharmaceutical, and government facilities, controls system security is a compliance requirement. Gardner Air tracks firmware versions across serviced controllers and flags systems approaching or past end of support.

Integration gaps between legacy and modern equipment

Facilities that add new HVAC equipment to an existing BAS often experience incomplete integration — new equipment visible on the network but not fully mapped to the supervisory layer, alarm conditions not configured, or trend logging not capturing the new points. Gardner Air identifies and closes integration gaps as part of the controls maintenance scope.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Commercial HVAC Controls Service

What is a building automation system and why does it need maintenance?

A building automation system (BAS) is a network of controllers, sensors, and software that monitors and controls a building's HVAC, lighting, and other mechanical systems. It maintains setpoints, executes control sequences, logs trends, and generates alarms when conditions fall outside defined parameters. A BAS requires maintenance because sensors drift from calibration, communication networks develop faults, PID loops require retuning as system conditions change, and firmware requires updates. A BAS that has not been maintained operates on inaccurate data and executes sequences against the last correct configuration — which may be years out of date.

What is the difference between BACnet, Modbus, and LonWorks?

BACnet is an open standard developed specifically for building automation and is the dominant commercial protocol for modern HVAC controls. It supports complex object models, alarm management, and flexible network topologies. Modbus is an older serial protocol widely used in industrial equipment, VFDs, meters, and legacy HVAC gear — simpler than BACnet but still common in commercial facilities. LonWorks is a peer-to-peer networking protocol deployed heavily in the 1990s and 2000s in institutional and campus buildings. Many facilities operate all three, with a supervisory BAS aggregating data across protocols. Gardner Air services all three and manages the integration between them.

How do I know if my BAS sensors are reading accurately?

The most reliable indicator is a calibration check against a reference instrument. Common field indicators of sensor drift include: spaces that feel warmer or cooler than the thermostat setpoint, energy consumption that has increased without a corresponding change in occupancy or weather, equipment that runs longer than expected to reach setpoint, and BAS trend data that shows setpoints being reached inconsistently. None of these indicators alone confirms sensor drift — they can also reflect mechanical issues — but sensor calibration should be verified before pursuing more costly mechanical interventions.

What is PID tuning and when does it need to be adjusted?

PID (proportional-integral-derivative) control is the algorithm most building automation controllers use to modulate HVAC equipment toward a setpoint. The P, I, and D parameters determine how aggressively the controller responds to a deviation from setpoint. Incorrect PID tuning causes equipment to overshoot the setpoint, oscillate, or hunt continuously without stabilizing. PID parameters need adjustment when HVAC equipment is replaced, when system loads change significantly, when a control loop that previously performed well begins exhibiting instability, or as part of a scheduled commissioning review. Gardner Air evaluates control loop performance on every maintenance visit.

Can you integrate new HVAC equipment with our existing BAS?

Gardner Air manages the integration of new HVAC equipment — chillers, air handlers, rooftop units, boilers — with existing BAS platforms across all major protocols. Integration work includes protocol configuration, point mapping, sequence of operations programming, alarm configuration, and trend logging setup. Where the new equipment uses a different communication protocol than the existing BAS, Gardner Air handles the protocol translation layer. Complete integration documentation is provided at the conclusion of every integration project.

What are signs my controls system needs immediate attention?

Controls conditions that warrant prompt attention include: equipment running continuously without cycling normally, setpoints that are not being maintained despite equipment operation, loss of communication alarms for multiple controllers, trend data that shows setpoints drifting over hours or days, alarm floods that are masking underlying fault conditions, and any situation where the BAS operator cannot verify that commands are executing at the field device level. Gardner Air's maintenance clients receive documented communications status and sensor calibration data after every visit, making early detection of these conditions possible before they escalate.
