## Refrigeration Systems We Service

### Walk-In Coolers and Freezers

Walk-in coolers and freezers are the foundation of cold storage in food service, grocery, and light industrial applications. The refrigeration system — typically a condensing unit matched to a unit cooler — must maintain precise temperature setpoints around the clock. Gardner Air's maintenance program covers condensing unit refrigerant charge verification, compressor diagnostics, condenser coil cleaning, unit cooler evaporator condition, defrost system timing and termination, door gasket integrity, and temperature control calibration. Both medium-temperature cooler and low-temperature freezer applications are within the program scope.

### Reach-In Refrigeration

Commercial reach-in refrigerators and freezers in food service, laboratory, and retail environments require the same disciplined maintenance approach as larger systems. Refrigerant charge, compressor operation, condenser coil fouling, and temperature control accuracy all affect product safety and operating cost. Gardner Air services stand-alone condensing units and reach-in cabinets within commercial facilities — not consumer appliances.

### Cold Storage Warehouse Systems

Large-scale cold storage warehouses operate refrigeration systems that can serve tens of thousands of square feet across multiple temperature zones. These systems typically use centralized rack systems or multiple distributed condensing units feeding overhead unit coolers, with ammonia or HFC refrigerant circuits sized for substantial heat loads. Gardner Air's maintenance approach for warehouse-scale refrigeration covers rack system compressor condition, oil management, refrigerant circuit integrity, defrost system performance across all zones, and the controls infrastructure that manages setpoints and alarm thresholds.

### Rack Refrigeration Systems

Supermarket-style rack refrigeration systems consolidate multiple compressors into a single mechanical room, serving both medium-temperature display cases and low-temperature frozen food cases through a common refrigerant circuit. These systems introduce complexity at the compressor rack, oil management system, suction manifold, and electronic controls level that exceeds the capability of generalist service providers. Gardner Air services both conventional and parallel-compression rack configurations, addressing compressor sequencing, oil separator function, superheat and subcooling values, and electronic controller programming.

### Low-Temperature vs. Medium-Temperature Applications

The distinction between low-temperature (freezer, typically -10°F to 0°F) and medium-temperature (cooler, typically 25°F to 45°F) refrigeration is not simply a setpoint difference. Low-temperature applications operate at suction saturation temperatures (SST) well below freezing, which changes compressor displacement requirements, oil return dynamics, and defrost cycle design. Medium-temperature systems operate with higher SST values and different evaporator approaches. Gardner Air's technicians apply the correct diagnostic benchmarks for each application type rather than treating all refrigeration systems as a single service category.

## Common Refrigeration Issues We Diagnose

- **Refrigerant leaks.** Even a small refrigerant leak reduces system capacity before it triggers an alarm. SST rises, compressor discharge temperature increases, and the box temperature follows. Gardner Air uses electronic leak detection to locate leaks before adding refrigerant, and documents leak points in the service report rather than masking the symptom with a charge addition.

- **Compressor short-cycling.** A compressor that starts, runs briefly, and stops repeatedly is working against a problem — high discharge pressure, low suction pressure, a faulty low-pressure switch, or a controls issue. Short-cycling accelerates mechanical wear and risks motor winding failure. Gardner Air diagnoses the cause on the first visit.

- **Defrost cycle failures.** Failed defrost heaters, defrost termination thermostats, or defrost controllers allow evaporator coil ice accumulation to restrict airflow and degrade heat transfer. The result is rising box temperature and compressor overwork as the system tries to compensate. Defrost system verification is a standard component of every Gardner Air maintenance visit.

- **Evaporator coil icing.** Ice accumulation on the evaporator coil beyond normal defrost cycles typically indicates low refrigerant charge, a defrost system fault, or an airflow restriction. Gardner Air identifies which of these conditions is causing the icing rather than simply defrosting the coil and leaving.

- **Condenser fan failures.** Condenser fan motor failure raises discharge saturation temperature (DST), increases compressor head pressure, and degrades capacity. In Southern California's summer ambient conditions, a failed condenser fan on a low-temperature system can trip high-pressure safeties within hours. Gardner Air inspects condenser fan motors and blade condition at every maintenance visit.

- **Temperature control drift.** A temperature controller operating on a miscalibrated or failed sensor delivers inaccurate setpoints — meaning the system may be running warmer or colder than the display indicates. For pharmaceutical cold rooms, laboratory samples, and food safety compliance, sensor accuracy is a regulatory matter. Gardner Air verifies controller calibration and sensor accuracy on every visit.

## FAQ

### How often should commercial refrigeration systems be serviced?

Commercial refrigeration systems operating around the clock — walk-ins, rack systems, cold storage — should receive preventative maintenance at minimum twice per year, with quarterly visits the appropriate interval for higher-criticality applications such as pharmaceutical cold rooms, laboratory freezers, and large food distribution operations. The correct frequency depends on system age, refrigerant type, operating hours, and the consequences of a temperature excursion. An annual-only interval for a system that runs 8,760 hours per year is not a defensible maintenance program.

### What is the difference between low-temperature and medium-temperature refrigeration?

Medium-temperature systems typically maintain box temperatures between 25°F and 45°F with suction saturation temperatures (SST) in the range of 0°F to 25°F. Low-temperature systems maintain box temperatures at or below 0°F, with SST values that may range from -30°F to -10°F. These operating conditions require different compressor displacement, different oil return strategies, and different defrost cycle designs. A compressor specified for medium-temperature operation cannot be applied to a low-temperature circuit without significant efficiency and reliability consequences. Gardner Air's technicians apply the correct diagnostic benchmarks and service procedures for each application type.

Gardner Air is current on the HFC phasedown schedule under AIM Act provisions and can advise on refrigerant transition planning for systems still operating on phased-down refrigerant types. Ammonia (R-717) systems in large cold storage applications are addressed on a case-by-case basis — contact Gardner Air directly to discuss your facility's configuration.

### How do I know if my commercial refrigeration system is underperforming?

The most direct indicators are box temperature running above setpoint, compressor running continuously without reaching setpoint, condenser unit cycling on high-pressure faults, unusual compressor noise, or frost accumulation on the evaporator coil outside of normal defrost cycles. Less obvious indicators include rising energy consumption for the same load, more frequent defrost cycles than normal, or superheat values outside of design range on refrigeration gauges. Gardner Air documents operating parameters on every maintenance visit specifically to make trend deviations visible before they become temperature excursions.

### What happens if a refrigerant leak goes undetected?

An undetected refrigerant leak reduces system refrigerant charge progressively. As charge drops, SST and superheat rise, compressor discharge temperature increases, and eventually the system loses enough capacity to fail temperature setpoints. Compressors running with insufficient refrigerant also run hot and risk motor winding failure. Beyond the mechanical damage, uncontrolled refrigerant emissions are an EPA regulatory matter for commercial facilities. Gardner Air uses electronic leak detection as part of every maintenance visit — not only when a problem is reported.
